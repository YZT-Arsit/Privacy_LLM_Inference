\section{Problem Formulation and Threat Model}
\label{sec:threat}

\subsection{System Model}
\label{subsec:system}

The deployment has three administrative parties. The \emph{Model Provider}
$\prov$ owns a proprietary decoder-only foundation model
$M_{\params}$ and provisions its plaintext parameters into the Cloud Service's
trusted execution environment (TEE). The \emph{User} $\usr$ owns private LoRA
training examples, labels, prompts, and context; the User also owns the
resulting adapter and generated result. The \emph{Cloud Service} $\cloud$
contains two execution components: the trusted TEE $\ctrl$ and an untrusted GPU
$\acc$. The TEE holds or transiently materializes plaintext secrets, controls
masks and pads, executes trusted operators and recovery, maintains LoRA and
optimizer state, recovers logits, decodes tokens, and returns results to the
User. The GPU performs delegated bulk computation on transformed operands.

The public architecture is
$\arch=(L,d,h,h_{kv},d_h,m,V)$. All learned model parameters are private:
$\params$ includes embeddings, projection and output-head weights,
normalization parameters, and learned biases when present. The GPU receives
only transformed images of these parameters; it never receives the plaintext
checkpoint.

\subsection{Protected Assets and Adversary View}
\label{subsec:attacker}

Protected Provider assets are $\params$. Protected adaptation assets are the
User's training examples, labels and targets, training prompts, LoRA factors,
gradients, and optimizer state. Protected generation assets are the prompt and
context, plaintext intermediate states, KV contents, pre-sampling logits,
generated token identities, and final result. The TEE releases the adapter or
generated result only to $\usr$.

For confidentiality, $\adv$ is the honest-but-curious GPU $\acc$: it follows
the delegated computation but records and correlates its transcript
$\transcript$ across calls and sessions. Its view comprises transformed
operands, the requested operation sequence, and admitted metadata: architecture
dimensions, tensor and batch shapes, sequence and cache lengths, padded rank
$\rpad$, call count, and the coarse operation schedule. Generated token
identities are not public metadata. The GPU cannot inspect the TEE or obtain
plaintext parameters, masks, pads, adapter factors, protected intermediate
states, logits, or results. Appendix~\ref{app:protocol} gives the full tensor
partition, freshness schedule, and message transcript.

\subsection{Goals and Non-Goals}
\label{subsec:objectives}

The functional goal is to recover the plaintext model computation at the TEE
boundary, including the delegated linear, autoregressive attention/cache, and
LoRA-adaptation paths. The confidentiality goal is empirical non-recovery of
the protected assets by the stated GPU adversary, subject to the admitted
metadata and transformed values. This is not a claim of cryptographic,
semantic, or differential-privacy indistinguishability.

The TEE is assumed uncompromised. Fine-grained timing, cache/page-fault, power,
physical, and other hardware side channels are outside the model. Availability
and malicious-GPU integrity are not goals; the implemented probabilistic
spot-check is an auxiliary audit rather than a general integrity guarantee.
Checkpoint disclosure, collusion that reveals TEE secrets, and unbounded
plaintext/transcript pairs are also outside scope.
