\section{Problem Formulation and Threat Model}
\label{sec:threat}

We specify the deployment setting, the trust boundary, the adversary, the
protection goals, and the assumptions we place outside our scope. This section
is a specification: the construction that realizes it is given in the Method
section, and all quantities are established in the Evaluation. Throughout,
parties are written in calligraphic type, tensors in bold, and sets of tensors
in blackboard type; all symbols are collected in the notation table.

\subsection{System Model}
\label{subsec:system}

\begin{definition}[Parties]
The deployment involves five parties. The \emph{model provider} $\prov$ owns a
proprietary foundation model with parameters $\params$. The \emph{user} $\usr$
owns an input token sequence $x=(x_1,\dots,x_n)$ and any private context. The
\emph{LoRA owner} $\loraown$ owns a low-rank adapter $(\Amat,\Bmat)$ and the
private data on which it is fine-tuned; $\loraown$ may coincide with $\usr$ or
$\prov$. The \emph{trusted controller} $\ctrl$ is a trusted execution domain.
The \emph{untrusted accelerator} $\acc$ is an external compute device.
\end{definition}

The model is a decoder-only Transformer $M_{\params}$ with public architecture
$\arch=(L,d,h,h_{kv},d_h,m,V)$: $L$ layers, model width $d$, $h$ query heads and
$h_{kv}$ key/value heads of dimension $d_h$, feed-forward width $m$, and
vocabulary size $V$. The architecture $\arch$ and the token embedding /
untying convention are public. The parameters
$\params=\{\W^{(\ell)}_{\bullet},\rmsgain^{(\ell)}\}_{\ell=1}^{L}$ --- the
per-projection weights $\W$ and the normalization gains $\rmsgain$ --- are
\emph{private and proprietary} and constitute intellectual property of $\prov$.
Where a projection carries a bias, the bias $\bias$ is a public constant.

\begin{definition}[Deployment]
\label{def:deployment}
$\ctrl$ holds $\params$, the adapter $(\Amat,\Bmat)$, and all secret randomness,
and is the only party that ever materializes plaintext runtime tensors. $\acc$
performs the bulk dense linear algebra on \emph{transformed} operands supplied by
$\ctrl$ and returns transformed results. The two communicate over a single
logical $\ctrl$--$\acc$ interface. A computation (one forward pass for inference,
or one forward--backward--update step for adaptation) is expressed as an
alternation of trusted operations inside $\ctrl$ and delegated linear operations
on $\acc$.
\end{definition}

\subsection{Trust Boundary}
\label{subsec:boundary}

Let the \emph{runtime state} of a computation be the collection of all tensors it
produces. The trust boundary partitions this state into four disjoint sets.

\begin{definition}[Trust partition]
\label{def:partition}
The runtime state is partitioned as $\Ssec \sqcup \Stra \sqcup \Sobs$, together
with public information $\Spub$:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Secret-resident} $\Ssec$: tensors that \emph{never} leave $\ctrl$.
  $\Ssec=\{\params,\ \maskset,\ \Tpad,\ (\Amat,\Bmat),\ \text{optimizer state},\
  \Recover\}$, where the mask family
  $\maskset=\{\Nin,\Nout,\NQ,\NK,\NV,\Rmask,\Perm,\Umix\}$ is drawn freshly per
  call (boundary and rank masks) or per session (the key/value masks
  $\NK,\NV$), $\Tpad$ is the one-time additive pad, and $\Recover$ is the trusted
  recovery map. These are \emph{permanently secret}.
\item \textbf{Transient plaintext} $\Stra$: tensors materialized inside $\ctrl$
  during a pass and erased afterward; they never cross the boundary.
  $\Stra=\{\X,\ \Qmat,\K,\Vmat,\ \text{pre-sampling logits},\ \Gmat,\ d\Amat,\
  d\Bmat\}$, where $\X$ denotes the plaintext hidden states, $\Qmat,\K,\Vmat$ the
  attention projections, and $\Gmat,d\Amat,d\Bmat$ the upstream and adapter
  gradients.
\item \textbf{Observable} $\Sobs$: the transformed images that cross to $\acc$.
  $\Sobs=\{\Xt,\Wt,\At,\Bt,\Kt,\Vt,\Yt\}$, with
  $\Xt=(\X-\Tpad)\Nin$, $\Wt=\Nin^{-1}\W\Nout$, $\At=\Nin^{-1}\Amat\Umix$,
  $\Bt=\Umix^{-1}\Bmat\Nout$, $\Kt=\K\NK$, $\Vt=\Vmat\NV$, and
  $\Yt=\Y\Nout$ (masked layer output). Cache entries are stored in their masked
  form $\Kt,\Vt$.
\item \textbf{Public} $\Spub$: information known a priori or disclosed by
  construction: the architecture $\arch$, biases $\bias$, the fixed chat
  template, tensor and batch shapes, the sequence length $n$, the key/value
  cache length, the padded LoRA rank $\rpad$, and the returned output tokens and
  their count.
\end{itemize}
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Transcript]
\label{def:transcript}
The \emph{accelerator transcript} $\transcript$ is the sequence of observable
tensors in $\Sobs$ together with the public metadata in $\Spub$ that $\acc$ sees
across a session, along with the sequence of linear operations $\acc$ is
instructed to compute. $\transcript$ is the entire view available outside
$\ctrl$.
\end{definition}

Thus a tensor is \emph{permanently secret} iff it lies in $\Ssec$, \emph{transient}
iff it lies in $\Stra$, and \emph{observable} iff it lies in $\Sobs$ or is part
of the public metadata; the true base weights $\W\in\params$ never appear in
$\transcript$, only their masked image $\Wt$.

\subsection{Threat Model}
\label{subsec:attacker}

\begin{definition}[Adversary]
\label{def:adversary}
The adversary $\adv$ is the untrusted accelerator $\acc$, together with any party
that observes the transcript $\transcript$. $\adv$ is \emph{honest-but-curious}:
it executes the delegated linear operations faithfully but may attempt to infer
protected information from $\transcript$.
\end{definition}

We characterize $\adv$ along four axes, which we keep explicitly distinct.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Passive observation.} $\adv$ reads the full transcript
  $\transcript$ and may apply unbounded offline computation to it, but does not
  alter the delegated computation. The confidentiality goals below are stated
  against a passive $\adv$.
\item \textbf{Chosen-input capability.} $\adv$ does not choose $\usr$'s inputs;
  it observes only the transcripts of inputs presented to the system. As a
  strictly stronger reference point we also define a \emph{bounded known-pairs}
  relaxation, in which $\adv$ is granted $K$ plaintext--transcript pairs
  $\{(\X_i,\Xt_i)\}_{i=1}^{K}$; this relaxation never grants $\params$ or
  $\maskset$.
\item \textbf{Model-execution observation.} $\adv$ observes the computation graph
  it is asked to evaluate over masked operands---i.e., the operation sequence,
  the shapes of every operand in $\Sobs$, and coarse per-call metadata such as
  the number of boundary crossings.
\item \textbf{Multi-query observation.} $\adv$ may store $\transcript$ and
  correlate observations across an unbounded number of calls and sessions. Mask
  freshness bounds cross-call linkage: boundary masks are redrawn per call, while
  the key/value masks are fixed within a session and independent across sessions.
\end{itemize}

\begin{definition}[Adversary knowledge and limits]
\label{def:knowledge}
$\adv$ knows the public information $\Spub$, the transformed package
$\{\Wt,\At,\Bt\}$, and the delegated operation sequence. $\adv$ does \emph{not}
obtain the plaintext parameters $\params$, the masks $\maskset$ or their inverses,
the pad $\Tpad$, any transient tensor in $\Stra$, or the plaintext adapter
$(\Amat,\Bmat)$, and cannot read the internal state of $\ctrl$.
\end{definition}

\subsection{Security Objectives}
\label{subsec:objectives}

We state the protection goals as objectives $O_1$--$O_7$, each defined relative to
the partition of Definition~\ref{def:partition} and the adversary of
Definition~\ref{def:adversary}. We separate correctness from confidentiality and never
conflate the two.

\begin{description}
\item[$O_1$ Correctness.] For every input, the value returned by $\ctrl$ equals
  that of the plaintext model $M_{\params}$: applying the recovery map
  $\Recover$ to the delegated masked execution reproduces the pre-sampling
  logits of $M_{\params}$, and hence its output token sequence under a fixed
  decoding rule. The precise numerical sense of this equality is stated in the
  Correctness section.
\item[$O_2$ Confidentiality.] For each protected asset
  $a\in\Ssec\cup\Stra$, the ability of $\adv$ to recover $a$ from the transcript
  $\transcript$ is no greater than from the public information $\Spub$ alone;
  equivalently, $\transcript$ confers no advantage beyond the leakage already
  admitted by $\Sobs$ and $\Spub$. We instantiate $O_2$ per asset:
  \emph{input} confidentiality of $x$ and $\X$; \emph{weight} confidentiality of
  $\params$; \emph{adapter} confidentiality of $(\Amat,\Bmat)$;
  \emph{gradient} confidentiality of $\Gmat,d\Amat,d\Bmat$ and the fine-tuning
  data; and \emph{cache} confidentiality of the values $\K,\Vmat$. We do not
  target cryptographic or semantic indistinguishability; $O_2$ is a
  non-recoverability goal under the stated adversary, and the extent to which it
  is met is established in the security analysis.
\item[$O_3$ Integrity.] $\ctrl$ can detect, with bounded failure probability, any
  deviation of $\acc$ from the specified linear computation.
\item[$O_4$ Availability.] Availability is \emph{not} a protection goal; it is
  named here only to delimit scope.
\item[$O_5$ Training privacy.] $O_2$ holds throughout the adaptation loop for the
  training-time assets: the fine-tuning data, the gradients
  $\Gmat,d\Amat,d\Bmat$, the adapter $(\Amat,\Bmat)$, and the optimizer state.
\item[$O_6$ Runtime privacy.] $O_2$ holds throughout inference for the runtime
  assets: the prompt $x$, the hidden states $\X$, and the cache values
  $\K,\Vmat$.
\item[$O_7$ Generation privacy.] Over the course of decoding, $\acc$ learns no
  more than the admitted leakage: the pre-sampling logits and the sampled token
  identities are transient or secret with respect to $\acc$, whereas the output
  length is admitted public information. The output tokens are disclosed to
  $\usr$ by definition, never to $\acc$.
\end{description}

\subsection{Out-of-Scope Assumptions}
\label{subsec:scope}

The following are assumptions delimiting the model; we state them without
argument.
\begin{itemize}
\item[$A_1$] The trusted controller $\ctrl$ is uncompromised: the confidentiality
  and integrity of its internal state ($\Ssec,\Stra$) hold. A malicious, leaky,
  or faulty controller is out of scope.
\item[$A_2$] The proprietary checkpoint $\params$ is never disclosed in plaintext
  to $\adv$; checkpoint-disclosure and public-weight settings are out of scope.
\item[$A_3$] Micro-architectural and physical side channels---fine-grained
  timing, power, electromagnetic emanation, cache and page-fault patterns, and
  resource contention---are out of scope; only the coarse per-call metadata of
  Definition~\ref{def:knowledge} is modeled.
\item[$A_4$] Physical attacks on the host are out of scope.
\item[$A_5$] Availability and denial-of-service are out of scope.
\item[$A_6$] Collusion that grants $\adv$ the plaintext parameters, the masks, or
  an unbounded set of plaintext--transcript pairs is out of scope; the bounded
  known-pairs relaxation of Section~\ref{subsec:attacker} is the only admitted
  leakage of this kind.
\item[$A_7$] For confidentiality, $\adv$ is honest-but-curious; an actively
  deviating accelerator is addressed only to the extent of objective $O_3$.
\end{itemize}
