\section{Problem Formulation and Threat Model}
\label{sec:threat}

\subsection{System Model}
\label{subsec:system}

The deployment has three administrative parties. The \emph{Model Provider}
$\prov$ owns a proprietary foundation model $M_{\params}$ and provisions its
private parameters into the Cloud Service's trusted execution environment
(TEE). The \emph{User} $\usr$ owns private LoRA data and adapter, generation
prompts and context, and generated results. The \emph{Cloud Service} $\cloud$
contains the trusted TEE $\ctrl$ and an untrusted GPU $\acc$ as execution
components. The TEE manages plaintext secrets, masks, pads, trusted operators,
recovery, LoRA and optimizer state, logits, decoding, and result return. The GPU
performs delegated bulk computation only on transformed operands.

\subsection{Assets and Adversary}
\label{subsec:attacker}

Protected Provider assets are all learned checkpoint parameters, including
embeddings, normalization parameters, output heads, and learned biases.
Protected User assets are LoRA examples, targets, factors, gradients and
optimizer state, prompts and context, plaintext activations and KV contents,
pre-sampling logits, generated token identities, and final results. Results and
adapters are released only to $\usr$.

The confidentiality adversary is the honest-but-curious GPU: it follows the
protocol but records and correlates the transcript of transformed operands,
delegated operations, and admitted metadata. Admitted metadata is limited to
architecture dimensions, tensor shapes, sequence/cache lengths, padded rank,
call count, and coarse schedule. The GPU cannot inspect the TEE and receives no
plaintext checkpoint, User input, adapter, protected activation/cache, logit,
token identity, or result. The Supplement formalizes the complete state
partition, transcript, freshness schedule, and stronger-query variants.

\subsection{Goals and Scope}
\label{subsec:objectives}

Correctness requires plaintext-equivalent execution; confidentiality is
empirical non-recovery from the correlated GPU transcript, not cryptographic
security. We exclude TEE compromise, hardware side channels, denial of service,
and malicious integrity; the GPU receives no plaintext reference checkpoint in
the canonical private-model threat model, so anchor-dependent alignment attacks
are outside scope and we evaluate transcript-only attacks.
